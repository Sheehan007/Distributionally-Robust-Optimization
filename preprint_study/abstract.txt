Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) addresses uncertainty about a return law in addition to risk under that law. This paper reviews established DRO methods and evaluates a reproducible portfolio pipeline without claiming a new optimization method. A persistence-anchored residual LSTM predicts changes in log rolling volatility. Physical Heston parameters are fitted annually to past returns using a latent-variance particle likelihood. A four-ETF mean-CVaR problem combines a Wasserstein penalty with quadratic risk. A preliminary single-asset analysis over 2019-2024 motivated the design, so that period is exploratory; 2025 supplies a separate retrospective confirmation period. Residual forecasting lowers RMSE against persistence by 16-21% in exploration and 22-29% in confirmation, although a simple rolling-window update remains competitive. Fitted Heston models have mixed predictive likelihood gains. A matched 20,000-scenario comparison under three independent seeds reduces Monte Carlo variation below the average robustness tilt, while robust-versus-nominal return differences remain statistically inconclusive. Strong 2025 performance is interpreted in light of a gold rally and concentrated gold holdings in a historical benchmark. A cash-enabled comparison separates asset composition from de-risking. The public release provides code, model records, aggregate results, and input hashes; numerical replication is conditional on access to matching provider inputs.
